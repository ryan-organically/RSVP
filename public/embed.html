Focal · embed

Put a reader on your page

One script tag adds a "Read in Focal" button to any article, chapter sample, or documentation page. Your readers get a fast, focused way through your text. You get the dwell time.

The whole install

<script src="https://focal.wiki/embed.js" data-focal-selector="article"></script>

Drop it before </body>. The button appears at the top of the matched element. That is the entire integration.

On reading faster than you speak

Most people read at roughly the speed they talk, and for the same reason. The voice in your head is not a metaphor. It is a real motor process, and it caps you somewhere near three hundred words a minute no matter how quickly your eyes could otherwise move.

Rapid serial visual presentation removes one half of that problem. Words arrive one at a time, in a fixed spot, so your eyes stop jumping and stop travelling back. What remains is the voice, and the honest research is split on how much of it you can drop before comprehension follows.

So the useful claim is narrower than the one usually made. RSVP will not turn you into a machine that absorbs a book in ten minutes. It will let you move through text at a pace you choose, without your eyes doing the work of finding the next word, and for a great deal of reading that is exactly the thing you wanted.

Opens in a new tab. The text travels in the URL fragment, which browsers never send to a server, so this page's words never touch ours.

What it does not do

  • No upload. Extraction happens in the visitor's browser and the text is handed straight to the reader.
  • No cookies, no local storage on your domain, no fingerprinting.
  • No network request at all until somebody clicks the button.
  • No account, for you or for your readers.
  • No analytics by default. If you want the dwell-time numbers, you opt in explicitly, and the counters are aggregate only.

This is not a privacy posture bolted on afterward. There is no path by which your readers' data could reach us, because the button never sends any.

Placing it yourself

<script src="https://focal.wiki/embed.js" data-focal-auto="false"></script>
<script>
  // Inject wherever you like
  Focal.inject({ mount: '#my-toolbar', label: 'Read this chapter' });

  // Or wire your own control
  document.querySelector('#read').onclick = () => Focal.open();

  // Or just take the extracted text
  const { title, text } = Focal.extract(document.querySelector('article'));
</script>

The button inherits your page's font and colour, and draws a one pixel border in the current text colour, so it sits inside your design instead of fighting it. Override .focal-btn in your own CSS if you want something else.

Long texts

Fragments handle most articles comfortably. A full chapter or a whole book is passed directly from your page to the reader tab instead, window to window, still without a server in the middle. You do not need to configure anything, the script picks the right path by size.

The read API

If you would rather send a link than extract text, the reader can fetch public documents itself:

GET https://focal.wiki/api/read?url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading
GET https://focal.wiki/api/read?url=https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762&format=text

Returns { source, url, title, byline, words, chars, text }. It stores nothing and requires no key. Sources currently served:

wikipedia (all languages) arxiv biorxiv medrxiv pubmed central gutenberg standard ebooks

The allowlist is deliberate rather than lazy. An endpoint that fetches any URL you name is a server-side request forgery tool, so Focal serves named sources only. Want yours added? Ask.
